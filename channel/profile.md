Lessons:
- **Story and conflict win.** A countdown with reactions (Andie Elle) beat everything. "Hottest looks" compilations flopped (Sweeney 68, Tate McRae 358).
- **Famous names alone don't carry a video.** Search interest is high, but so is competition. You need an angle.
- Shorts were reposts of the long-form and never had their own hooks.

## Positioning (going forward)
**ModelVault = the internet-fame channel.** It covers how creators, influencers and celebrities blew up, the viral moments, the drama, the money, the downfalls, and the AI models taking their jobs.

Audience: mostly men aged 18–34 who follow streamer, TikTok and IG culture and know the names. They want the inside story and the "wait, what?" moment, told fast.

### Content pillars
1. **Rise / Downfall docs.** "How X went from Y to Z", "The downfall of X". Proven outlier format on other channels.
2. **Viral countdowns.** "Top 10 craziest moments from X's series". This is our best-performing format.
3. **Drama explained.** Bop House, dating shows, streamer beef. A timeline with receipts.
4. **AI influencers.** Fake models, the money behind them, can you tell? It's evergreen and growing, and it's safe to monetize.
5. **Money & business of fame.** What creators actually earn and how the deals work.

### Voice
Fast, a little cheeky, a friend telling you the tea. Short sentences. Specific numbers. No "hey guys". No moralizing. Roast the situation, not the person's body.

## Red lines (non-negotiable)
- No sexualizing real people. No "hottest pics", body-ranking or thirst compilations. Fashion and red-carpet coverage is fine as fashion.
- Never feature anyone under 18, and never anyone whose age you can't verify.
- Allegations get framed as allegations, with a source ("according to her own TikTok…", "fans accused…"). Never state a crime or scandal as fact unless it's on the record.
- No doxxing and no private info, even when it's floating around online.
