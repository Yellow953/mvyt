# YouTube upload & channel optimization

ModelVault publishes **only on YouTube**. Every packaging decision optimizes for YouTube's surfaces: Home/Browse, Suggested, Search, and the Shorts Feed.

## How YouTube judges a video (the long-form ranking lane)
- **CTR × AVD (average view duration) × satisfaction.** The thumbnail and title earn the click, the first 30s keep it, and the payoff makes viewers satisfied. YouTube also uses viewer surveys, so clickbait that doesn't deliver gets suppressed.
- Most growth for small channels comes from **Suggested** (showing next to similar videos). Packaging close to proven outliers in our niche, plus clear topic consistency, gets us suggested alongside them.
- A new video is tested first on subscribers and similar viewers, then widened if CTR and retention beat the channel's baseline.
- The **Shorts Feed is a separate lane** (see shorts.md). Shorts don't hurt long-form, but they also don't automatically feed it. The bridge has to be built (below).

## Upload checklist: long-form
- **Title:** see packaging.md. Front-load the name or hook word. Use 40–60 characters and no hashtags.
- **Thumbnail:** upload 3 variants with **Test & Compare** (YouTube's native A/B test, which picks by watch time). Use 1280×720 under 2MB.
- **Description:**
  - The first 2 lines restate the hook and carry the main search phrase. They show above the fold.
  - Then **chapters** (`0:00 Intro` first, each at least 10s, at least 3 chapters). Chapters show in search and in Key Moments.
  - Then "Sources:" with links. This builds credibility and helps with copyright disputes.
  - Then 2–3 hashtags at the end. The first 3 show above the title.
- **Tags:** low weight. Add the name, its common misspellings, and the topic.
- **End screen** (last 5–20s): a "best for viewer" video plus subscribe. Script the outro so it points at the end-screen video.
- **Cards:** one card where the script mentions a related video.
- **Playlist:** add it to the matching pillar playlist (Downfalls, Countdowns, Drama Explained, AI Influencers). Playlists help session time and Suggested.
- **Pinned comment:** a question that drives replies, matching the script's comment question.
- **Audience:** "Not made for kids". **Altered/synthetic content disclosure:** tick it only if we show realistic AI-generated people or events as real. AI narration alone doesn't require it.
- **Upload time:** schedule for when the audience is online (Studio → Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube"). The first 24–48h matter.

## Upload checklist: Shorts
- 9:16, ≤60s (we target 30–45s), with title text ≤40 characters that works as a hook.
- **Related video link:** in the Shorts upload settings, link to the long-form. This is the official Shorts → long-form bridge and shows as a tappable link on the Short. Always set it.
- The pinned comment repeats the link line.
- Batch Shorts by topic around each long-form launch: 2–3 Shorts in the days after a long-form, all linking to it.

## Channel-level setup (one-time, do with the user)
- A banner and channel description that state the new positioning (internet-fame stories: rise, drama, downfall, AI models).
- A channel trailer, plus a featured section per pillar playlist on the Home tab.
- Shorts tab: pin the 3 best topical Shorts.
- Consistency: one long-form a week plus 3–5 Shorts beats bursts. Pick a cadence the pipeline can sustain.

## Monetization guardrails
- YPP requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days.
- **Reused / repetitious content policy:** clips must be transformed by our commentary, edit and point of view. A TTS slideshow of other people's clips gets rejected. Our template adds VO, captions, graphics and reframing, but the *script* must add real analysis.
- **Advertiser-friendly guidelines:** sexual thumbnails, profanity in the first 7s, and harsh drama language limit ads (yellow icon). Keep thumbnails and titles clean. Bleep or avoid swearing early.
- **Copyright:** short clips, commentary over them, and sources logged in `sources.md`. Respond to Content ID claims with the fair-use rationale only when the clip is truly transformed.
