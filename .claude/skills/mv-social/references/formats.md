# ModelVault formats (beat skeletons)

Pick one per long-form. Each has proven outliers (see `research/`). Update this file when research finds new winning formats.

## A. Rise & Fall doc ("The Downfall of X" / "How X Blew Up")
Proof: "The Satisfying Downfall of OnlyJayus" (7x channel median, 6.1M), "The END of Becca Bloom" (22x).
1. Cold open: the lowest (or highest) moment, mid-clip.
2. "But to understand how we got here…" Then the origin: a normal job or life (humanize it).
3. The breakout moment: the specific viral video, with numbers.
4. The peak: money, followers, deals, the specific flex.
5. Cracks: controversy #1, #2, #3, escalating. Every allegation gets attributed.
6. The turning point: the moment it fell apart.
7. Now: where they are today, with sourced numbers.
8. Verdict plus a comment question.

## B. Viral Countdown ("Top 10 craziest moments from X")
Proof: our own Andie Elle video (26K, the channel's #1).
1. Hook: a flash of the #1 moment (blurred or cut before the payoff), then "10 clips, one question for you at the end".
2. #10 is the third-strongest clip (open strong).
3. #9–#4: the setup line, the clip, a one-line reaction or roast, and a "but #N is worse" forward hook.
4. Re-hook before #3: "the top three got [specific consequence]".
5. #3, #2, #1, with escalating SFX and graphics.
6. A poll-style comment question.

## C. Drama Explained ("The [House/Show] Drama Explained")
Proof: "THE BOP HOUSE EXPOSED" (970, our drama best); similar formats outlie at 10x+ on drama channels.
1. Cold open: the most quotable line of the beef.
2. The cast in 30s (name cards with one-liners).
3. A timeline with dated receipts (screenshots, clips).
4. "Both sides": what each person claims, attributed.
5. What the internet thinks (Reddit/comment sentiment, quoted, no handles).
6. What happens next, plus a verdict question.

## D. AI Influencer Investigation
Proof: "Exposing the AI Video Influencer Scam" (63x), "How to create AI influencer" (463x), "AI influencer trend is getting disturbing" (9x).
1. Magician hook: show the model and let it sit, then "she's not real".
2. The reveal: how we know (artifacts, the agency, the disclosure).
3. The money: what these accounts earn and who's behind them, sourced.
4. A spot-the-AI segment (interactive and very rewatchable). This works great as a Short.
5. The implications for real creators and for fans.
6. Fortuneteller close.

## E. Money of Fame ("How much X really makes")
1. Number hook ("$X a month, and that's the *small* stream").
2. A breakdown by revenue stream: on-screen stat cards, each with a source.
3. A comparison ladder against peers.
4. The catch (costs, the agency cut, a ban risk).
5. Verdict.

## Short-native formats
- **One-fact Short:** a single shocking sourced fact plus a receipt plus a loop.
- **Spot the AI:** two faces, "one is AI", a 3s pause, the reveal. This loops naturally.
- **Before/after fame:** an old clip next to the current one, with a number.
- **Countdown micro:** 3 moments in 40s, #1 teased first.
- **Story in 40s:** the in-media-res version of a long-form, ending with "full story on the channel".
